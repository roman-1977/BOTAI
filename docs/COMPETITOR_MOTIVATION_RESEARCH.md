# Motivation research: learning, fitness and reading products

Status: Research input for BOTAI product design, 2026-09-26

## Patterns worth borrowing

### Learning products

Duolingo separates consistency (streak) from richer goals and adds opt-in social commitment through Friend Streaks and Friends Quests. The useful principle for BOTAI is not XP itself, but several independent reasons to return: personal continuity, a finite task, milestones, and cooperative accountability.

RemNote-like spaced-repetition products demonstrate another important split: a small habit threshold and the actual workload generated by due learning. BOTAI should not pretend those are the same number.

### Fitness products

Strava supports goals across weekly/monthly/annual periods and multiple metrics, suggested goals, progress views, streaks, cumulative monthly statistics, and comparison with prior periods. Apple Fitness combines today's progress, trends, awards/history and optional sharing, while letting users customize the summary.

The useful principle is a hierarchy: live progress today → period trend → personal record/milestone → long-term trajectory. Not every metric belongs on Home.

### Reading products

Bookly supports book-specific, daily, monthly and yearly goals; for a book goal it can calculate the daily pace needed to finish on time. Goodreads' Reading Challenge emphasizes an adjustable long-term target, achievements and optional friend visibility.

The useful principle for BOTAI is pace-to-deadline: the user states the destination; the product translates it into today's sustainable amount and recalculates when reality changes.

## BOTAI metric inventory

BOTAI may calculate many metrics while surfacing only a few at once:

- current daily study streak and longest streak;
- studied days this week/month;
- minimum-day completion;
- BOTAI plan completed today and across the week;
- questions completed today/week/month;
- study time today/week/month;
- correct/hard/incorrect distribution and accuracy;
- change versus previous week/month;
- change versus rolling personal baseline;
- new material introduced;
- weak items strengthened;
- due/backlog trend;
- personal records;
- pace toward a dated Goal;
- optional friend/challenge metric for the same period.

## Three-layer motivation model

1. **Keep momentum** — a deliberately small minimum that marks a meaningful study day and maintains the daily streak.
2. **Do today's plan** — BOTAI's adaptive workload derived from due/weak/new material and the active Goal. It can vary by day.
3. **Reach the destination** — long-term pace/readiness toward the user's Goal and deadline.

These layers must never be collapsed into one `10 questions` counter.

## Period comparison

Progress supports Today, Week, Month and longer history. Week and Month should explicitly compare with the immediately preceding equivalent period and, when useful, the learner's rolling baseline.

Copy should explain the change, for example: `126 questions, +18% vs last week`, `5 study days, +1`, `accuracy 85%, +4 pp`, `weak backlog 24, down 13`.

## Motivation feed

Home has a limited dynamic insight slot. Candidate events include: streak milestone, target almost complete, plan complete, personal record, meaningful week-over-week improvement, weak-backlog reduction, pace warning, or a relevant accepted challenge.

Selection should prioritize usefulness and novelty. It must not show five congratulations at once or manufacture a positive claim from tiny samples.

## Guardrails

No shame for a missed day; no accumulated debt; no automatic public leaderboard; no sharing of weak topics/raw Attempts; no deceptive `readiness` percentage before its algorithm has a defensible definition; and no engagement metric should reward low-quality rapid guessing.

## BOTAI synthesis

Patterns worth carrying into BOTAI rather than copying a competitor UI:

- separate habit continuity from the full recommended workload;
- compare week/month periods with the previous equivalent period;
- use activity calendar, streak and personal records as secondary motivation;
- make long-term pace visible when a dated Goal exists;
- keep Home focused on today's next action and move analytical depth to Progress;
- allow explicit social/challenge comparison without making a public leaderboard the default;
- surface a small number of contextual motivational signals instead of every metric at once.

The first visual storyboard validated the information architecture but is not a final visual specification. Screen-level hierarchy and component design remain iterative.
